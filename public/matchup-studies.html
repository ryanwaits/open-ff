Hum · matchup card

Four ways to end the header and start the roster

The card is structurally right — two rosters, paired by slot, with the tally on top. What it lacks is a seam. Below are four studies, all built from tokens already in src/styles.css. Nothing new is invented; only the arrangement changes.

  • 01Three rhythms, no rule. The title spans the card, the team names span a column, the rows span a column again. Nothing marks the change.
  • 02The seam is the same size as a row gap. mt-3 under the tally against space-y-1.5 between rows — so the score line reads as row zero.
  • 03The projections collide in the gutter. 108.3 and 98.0 sit adjacent with no axis between them, while the two live totals are pinned to opposite outer edges.
  • 04A 40px gutter holding nothing. The columns sit beside each other rather than against each other.
  • 05The slot label and the meta line are the same grey. Structure and incidental detail read at identical weight.
Now

The card as it ships

For comparison. Read the seam between 108.3 proj and Dak Prescott — there isn't one.

Study A

Scorebar

The tally lifts off the paper and becomes an inset tile on --paper-sunken, with a hairline axis running between the two sides. Separation by surface, not by line — the rosters then sit on plain paper below, unmistakably a different kind of thing. The centre rule also fixes fault 03: each projection now belongs to a side.

Cost: one wrapper div, one background token, one 1px column. No data change.

Study B

Ledger

Stays flat and does it with rules instead. A full-bleed hairline crosses the whole card under the tally, then a micro-caps strip labels what the columns actually hold — slot / player / proj. The strip does double duty: it names the columns and it is a second, lighter division. Rows get hairlines, so the roster reads as a table rather than a stack. Most literally "Ledger", which is what the stylesheet already calls itself.

Cost: a bleed rule (-mx-5), a header strip, divide-y on the list.

Study C

Spine

The dead gutter becomes the structure. One shared axis runs the height of the board, and the slot labels move into it — so QB appears once, between the two quarterbacks, instead of twice at the outer edges. The seam is simply where the spine begins. This is the arrangement MatchupSpine already uses on the phone, so the desktop and mobile views would finally be the same idea at two sizes.

Cost: the largest change — the two <ul>s merge into one row list. SideCol is no longer the unit.

Study D

Masthead

Title and tally fuse into one full-bleed band across the top of the card, the way a real box score is printed. "Your matchup" demotes to a mono eyebrow inside the band — it is a label, not a headline — and the team names take the weight it gives up. The band's bottom edge is the seam, and it runs the full card width so it cannot be mistaken for a row divider.

Cost: padding moves from the card to its two regions. Strongest read; least like the sibling cards on the page.

Study E · the build

Masthead Spine

D's band with C's axis run through it. Because the band and the rows share one grid, the centre channel is continuous — vs sits directly above the stack of slot labels without needing rules of its own to say so. The band's lower edge is the seam; the channel's hairlines start where the rosters do, and the channel is what the two sides are measured against. Slot labels appear nine times instead of eighteen.

Cost: SideCol is replaced by a paired row list. The card's padding moves onto its two regions.

Study E · live

The same board, mid-game

Every row is the same height whether or not a gain landed on it this poll, because the gain line and the stat line both hold their space. The mark for “this just scored” is the gain itself, in accent green, over a 15% breath of the highlighter. During a sim most cells score at once, so anything heavier inverts — the un-lit cells start reading as the exception. It is not — not the old bg-win/10, which resolved to 10% ink and greyed the cell out.

Where this lands

A–D are the four separate arguments; E is the one being built — D's masthead band with C's axis run through it. The merge is worth more than either half: sharing one grid between the band and the rows turns the centre gutter into a single channel that starts at the scoreboard and ends at the card's edge, so the seam and the axis are the same gesture rather than two competing ones.

It also clears all five faults at once — one horizontal rhythm, a full-bleed seam that cannot read as a row divider, an axis that assigns each projection to a side, a gutter carrying the slot labels, and structure that no longer competes with the meta line for the same grey.
